# Judging disagreements

You're helping decide whether a new classifier can replace the current one. Most of
the texts you'll see are ones where the two systems disagree, and a few are ones where
they agree but one of them was unsure. You won't be told which system said what, or
which kind of record you're looking at, so judge each text on its own.

## How to answer

- **Pick the label the definitions call for.** Read the label definitions shown with
  each text and choose the one that fits, as if you were the only one labelling it.
  Don't guess what either system would say.
- **Use the text as shown.** If the text doesn't give you enough to decide, that's a
  real answer: choose the label your definitions have for that case (e.g. "needs
  clarification"), or UNCLEAR if they have none.
- **Choose UNCLEAR when you could defend more than one label** under the definitions
  as written. These are the most useful answers: each one points at a definition that
  needs a decision, and the fix is usually one sentence in the instructions.
- **Don't use UNCLEAR for texts you find hard but can still decide.** Pick the best
  label instead.

## Noting patterns

As you go, keep a short list of the kinds of text that made you hesitate, with an
example of each: jargon you had to look up, replies that only make sense with more
context, a business rule you applied that the definitions don't state. These become
the rules added to the classifier's instructions.
